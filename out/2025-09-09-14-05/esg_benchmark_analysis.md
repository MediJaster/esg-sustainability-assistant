# ESG Benchmark Analysis for Ernst & Young (EY)  
### Prepared for: Executive Leadership | Date: June 2024

---

## 1. Executive Summary  

**Key Insight 1:**  
EY demonstrates robust ESG leadership with advanced targets on carbon neutrality, DEI, and governance, aligning closely with sector best practices set by front-runners BCG and PwC. However, EY’s relative lag in high-granularity Scope 3 supply chain emissions data and external reporting transparency, especially compared to PwC’s supplier engagement, marks the primary gap.

**Key Insight 2:**  
The most critical priority for EY is to further operationalize Scope 3 decarbonization and supply chain circularity while deepening integration of real-time, assured ESG data platforms—this is where regulatory pressure and client demands are converging most sharply across the sector.

**Key Insight 3:**  
EY is competitively positioned in governance and wellbeing/DEI but needs to reinforce its “Sustainability-as-a-Service” and digital ESG capabilities to maintain leadership as the consulting industry transitions rapidly toward recurring analytics- and platform-integrated ESG offerings.

## 3. Top 2 Priority ESG Areas & Rationale

### **Priority Area 1: Scope 3 Emissions & Supply Chain Circularity**
**Rationale:**  
- Scope 3 represents the bulk of consulting sector CO2. Sector leaders (e.g., PwC) are targeting >30% supplier engagement with Science Based Targets and have deep, auditable tracking.
- EU and UK regulations in 2024–2025 will require far greater transparency, including for consulting services (Scope 3, chain-of-custody).
- ESG-conscious clients now view Scope 3 data as criteria in procurement; lag here exposes EY to competitive and regulatory risk.
- Circular economy approaches (e.g., digital product passports, waste minimization) are expected by key industries and embedded in top clients’ own ESG demands.

### **Priority Area 2: Digital/AI-enabled Real-Time ESG Data & Reporting**
**Rationale:**  
- Global top clients expect "always-on" ESG dashboards, not annual PDF disclosures; digital/AI oversight soon will be a baseline.
- BCG/PwC are early adopters of "Sustainability-as-a-Service" and integrated assurance-analytics products, deepening client “stickiness” and share of wallet.
- This area offers new recurring revenue models and cements EY’s positioning as both advisor and solution provider. It also supports compliance with next-gen regulation mandating audit-grade, real-time data (EU CSRD, SEC rules).
- Closing the tech/analytics gap is critical to both defend key accounts and win in competitive RFPs.

## 4. Recommended ESG Metrics Set (For Internal Tracking & External Benchmarking)

| Metric                      | Why Critical?                          | Target/Benchmark Level               |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Scope 3 GHG Emissions (mtCO2e, absolute and per FTE)** | High materiality (business travel, supply chain); underpins carbon strategy and regulatory/client mandates. | >40% reduction (by 2025 vs. 2019 baseline); aim for sector median or better (<1500kg/FTE/year for travel by 2026). |
| **% Suppliers with Science-Based Targets (SBTi or equivalent)** | Direct lever on supply chain carbon/circularity improvement; key procurement criterion. | 30%+ supplier engagement by mid-2025 (align with PwC best practice).             |
| **DEI Leadership Pipeline (% women & minority partners/principals)** | Core metric for both talent and client ESG requirements; underpins social credibility and RFP success. | 35%+ women in partner roles (by 2025); parity with BCG/PwC on minority representation (US/UK: >20%). |
| **ESG Assurance Coverage (KPIs with external, third-party attestation)** | ESG credibility and trust now hinge on regular, independent audits of disclosed performance data. | 100% of headline ESG KPIs (Scope 1–3, DEI targets, supply chain ethics) externally assured by 2025. |

Optionally, a **fifth metric**:  
**% Revenue from Digital/Recurring ESG Services** (to track transformation from traditional advisory to platform-based offerings; peer baseline ~10–15% of ESG revenue via digital or managed services by 2025).

## 8. Conclusions & Recommendations (Actionable Steps Summary)

**A. Address supply chain and Scope 3/circularity data**
- Expand SBTi-aligned supplier engagement from current levels to >30% of total supplier spend by 2025.
- Build sector-competitive granularity in Scope 3 emissions, including downstream client impact analytics and reporting (match PwC/BCG).

**B. Accelerate digital ESG platform and data analytics deployment**
- Prioritize scaling “Sustainability-as-a-Service” platforms for internal and client use, integrating real-time dashboards, AI-enabled anomaly detection, and benchmarking.
- Externally promote digital capabilities and market as proof points during ESG consulting RFPs.

**C. Expand external assurance and governance impact**
- Move to 100% externally assured disclosure of all material ESG KPIs.
- Spotlight and communicate advisory vs. assurance independence via explicit third-party governance branding.

**D. Continue to expand DEI and wellbeing innovations**
- Maintain global progress on women/minority partner appointments.
- Leverage wellbeing champions/mental health initiatives as differentiators in EY employer brand.

**E. Recommended Metrics (track and publish externally):**
1. Scope 3 emissions per FTE and as % of total—target 40%+ reduction from 2019
2. % of suppliers with SBTi or equivalent targets—target >30%
3. % women/minorities in partner/principal role—target 35%/20%
4. % of ESG KPIs with external (third-party) assurance—target 100%
   
*Optional:* % of ESG revenues/services delivered via digital/recurring platforms—target 10–15%+ by 2025

--- 

**In summary:**  
EY is strongly positioned but must “double down” on Scope 3/circularity engagement and digital product transformation to retain leadership in a sector where regulatory pressure and digital innovation are redefining ESG excellence. Focused investment and transparent external communication in these areas will move EY from a solid performer to an unquestioned best-in-class ESG leader.
